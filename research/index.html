  • Paper: On the effects of similarity metrics in decentralized deep learning under distributional shift Co-authored with Edvin Listo Zec, Tom Hagander and Sarunas Girdzijauskas
    2025 TMLR

    This paper asks how much the choice of similarity metric matters when peers must identify each other using only their models. Across four kinds of distribution shift on synthetic data, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, we found that the commonly used inverse-loss metric is noisy with small datasets, cosine similarity of weights or gradients is more robust, and L2 distance is usually weakest. We also introduced FedSim, which weights merged models by similarity instead of dataset size, improving results when merging with the wrong peer is costly.

  • Master’s thesis: With a Little Help from My Friends: A Comparative Study of Decentralized Deep Learning Strategies Co-authored with Tom Hagander
    2024 Lund University

    Decentralized deep learning lets participants with private datasets, such as hospitals or phone users, improve their neural networks by merging models directly with each other, without sharing data or relying on a central server. But when participants’ data differ, merging with the wrong peer can hurt. My thesis compares strategies for choosing good collaborators: two communication protocols (DAC and PANM), four model-based similarity metrics, and several ways of merging models, evaluated on heterogeneous data.
